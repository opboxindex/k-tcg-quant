How many packs are in a One Piece booster box?

A standard One Piece Card Game booster box contains 24 booster packs. That is true for the Japanese and English versions of every main set (OP-01 through the current release). What differs between the two languages is what is inside each pack — and what you pay for it.

Japanese vs English boxes: the official numbers

  • Japanese booster pack: 6 cards per pack (Bandai official). A 24-pack box holds 144 cards. For the newest set, OP-17 The World's Strongest Warriors, Bandai lists an MSRP of ¥240 per pack (tax included); its page states no box configuration — at the standard 24 packs that is ¥5,760, about $36.40 at ¥1 = $0.00632 (November 4, 2026). Earlier sets launched at lower pack prices, so check the official product page for a specific set's original MSRP.
  • English booster pack: Bandai's US site lists current English boosters at USD $4.99 MSRP per pack — about $119.76 per 24-pack box. On Bandai's English product pages, the EB-01 page lists 12 cards per pack; the EB-05 page states no per-pack card count (checked October 14, 2026); the English main-set pages give no per-pack card count.
  • Boxes per case: a case is a sealed carton of boxes shipped to retailers. For the Japanese OP-01 Romance Dawn display, a retailer source lists 12 boxes per case (onepiececard-letter.com). Bandai's product pages do not state a case configuration, so this is not generalized to other sets.

Two boxes, same set, very different sticker prices: a Japanese box at retail costs roughly a third of an English box at retail. This gap at MSRP is the starting point for the much larger market-price gaps we track — see our six-month Japanese vs English price study, and the live English÷Japanese multiple for every set in the booster box price guide.

What rarities come in a box?

Bandai publishes how many card types each rarity has, but never the odds of pulling them. The newest Japanese main set, OP-17 The World's Strongest Warriors, breaks down like this on Bandai's own product page:

Source: Bandai's official OP-17 product page, read October 14, 2026. These are counts of distinct cards in the set, not how many land in one box. A pack holds 6 cards and Bandai publishes no pull rates, so any precise "1 in X packs" figure is somebody's estimate.

How many of the top rarities land in any given box is randomized, and premium variants (manga art, special-art parallels) appear at low, unpublished odds. Community case-opening reports circulate, but the figures conflict widely between sources, so this site does not publish a pull rate until an auditable sealed-case sample supports it.

Collectors often compare chase-card prices with sealed-box prices because rare premium variants are part of opening demand. The comparison is not expected value: Bandai does not publish the pull rates needed for that calculation. Practical takeaways:

  • Buying to open: compare the box price to MSRP first. Paying far above retail for an in-print set means the market expects chase cards to justify it — verify that on the set's top-10 card list before you pay the premium.
  • Buying to hold: the number of packs never changes; what changes is scarcity. Our comparison table shows every box's current price, card support and scarcity signals side by side.
  • Avoiding incomplete or resealed boxes: verify that a main-set display contains 24 packs and that its original closure matches the edition. For Japanese displays, prioritize uncut original Bandai tape rather than added shrink wrap. See the reseal checklist.

Special box types: EB and PRB

Besides main sets, Bandai releases Extra Boosters (EB) — such as EB-01 Memorial Collection and EB-03 Heroine's Edition — and Premium Boosters (PRB), reprint-focused products sold mainly through Japanese channels. Their per-pack contents and pricing differ from main sets and from each other, so check the official product page for each. On the market side they behave like main sets: we track their sealed box prices the same way (PRB-01 was the single strongest Japanese box of early 2026, +113% in six months).

FAQ

How many packs are in a One Piece booster box?

A standard One Piece Card Game booster box contains 24 booster packs. This applies to Japanese and English main-set boxes (OP-01 onward).

How many cards are in a full Japanese booster box?

24 packs × 6 cards = 144 cards per Japanese box.

Is a case the same as a box?

No. A case is a sealed carton of boxes shipped to retailers. A retailer source lists 12 boxes per case for the Japanese OP-01 display; Bandai does not publish case configurations. This site tracks single sealed boxes only — case listings are excluded from the price data.

Do all boxes guarantee a Secret Rare or manga rare?

No. Bandai does not guarantee a manga-art card in each box, and the relevant pull rates are not published. Card demand, sealed supply and that uncertainty can all affect secondary-market prices.
